Brand & Style Guidelines

A working reference for designing and building brand-compliant SkySlope experiences — sites, product UI, decks, and campaigns.

Version 1.0 August 2026 Digital-first

Provenance note: This is an independently compiled reference, reconstructed from SkySlope’s live public web properties (skyslope.com and subpages) on August 12, 2026 — not an official SkySlope publication. Values marked “observed” were extracted directly from the site’s CSS and assets; values marked “derived” are recommended conventions inferred from consistent usage. For canonical assets and approvals, contact SkySlope’s marketing team.

01Brand at a glance

Who SkySlope is

SkySlope builds transaction management software for real estate brokerages — one intelligent platform that runs from contract to payout. Founded in 2011 in Sacramento, CA (part of the Fidelity National Financial family since 2017), SkySlope serves 900,000+ real estate professionals across the U.S. and Canada.

Positioning

The current masthead: “The AI-first way to run a brokerage.” Supporting line: “SkySlope brings intelligent automation to every transaction—so teams move faster with less risk.” The brand promise pairs intelligence (AI, automation, anticipation) with trust (compliance, 24/7 award-winning support, scale).

Messaging pillars

PillarWhat it meansProof language (from site)

AI that works like your teamThe software learns, anticipates, and keeps deals moving.“…learns how your agents work, anticipates what’s next, and keeps everything moving forward.”
Speed with less riskAutomation accelerates work while compliance keeps pace.“Auditors move forward faster, and compliance keeps pace.”
Humans, 24/7Award-winning support is part of the product.“With 24/7 award-winning support, SkySlope streamlines workflows across desktop and mobile.”
Trusted at scaleThe engine behind the industry’s top brokerages.“The trusted engine behind real estate’s most successful brokerages”

Product family

The suite (always set in this capitalization): SkySlope (transaction management), Forms, DigiSign, Offers, Books, Breeze, SkyTC, SkySight (mobile app), Ayce (AI coaching), and the Smart Suite of AI features. The company blog is called SkyHigh.

Personality

  • Confident pilot — assured, in command, never arrogant.
  • Plain-spoken — short sentences, concrete benefits, zero enterprise jargon.
  • Energetic — momentum verbs: move, launch, streamline, keep pace.
  • Obsessed with customers — support is a feature, people are the point.
  • A little cosmic — the space motif adds wonder, used deliberately and sparingly.
02Logo

The mark of momentum

The SkySlope lockup pairs the “S” swoosh mark — two strokes suggesting sky and slope in motion — with an all-caps wordmark. The mark is always Science Blue; the wordmark is Slope Slate on light backgrounds and white on dark.

Approved variants

Clear space & minimum size

Keep a protective zone equal to the height of the “S” mark on all four sides — nothing enters it: no type, no imagery, no edges. (Derived convention; the site consistently floats the logo in generous space.)

Minimum sizes (derived): full lockup ≥ 120 px wide on screen / 30 mm in print; mark-only ≥ 20 px. Below these, letterforms and the swoosh strokes lose definition. The site renders the header lockup at 190 × 31 px.

Color rules

BackgroundMarkWordmark
White / Frost / Sky MistScience Blue #0059DASlope Slate #3F5B77
Mirage / Midnight Sky / space gradientsWhiteWhite
Science Blue / saturated brand colorWhiteWhite
PhotographyWhite, only over a dark or scrimmed area that keeps contrast ≥ 4.5:1
Do
  • Use the provided SVG assets at native proportions (153 : 25).
  • Default to the two-color lockup on white.
  • Switch to the all-white reverse on Space, Navy, or Science Blue.
  • Use the mark alone at small sizes (app icons, social avatars).
Don’t
  • Recolor, gradient-fill, outline, or add effects to the logo.
  • Stretch, condense, rotate, or crop it.
  • Set the wordmark in a different typeface or re-space the lockup.
  • Place the two-color version on dark or busy backgrounds.
  • Pair the blue mark with Space Navy (contrast is only 2.85 : 1).

Proportions

Compositions on the site hold roughly to 60 / 30 / 10: ~60% white and light neutrals (content zones), ~30% space navies (hero and feature moments), ~10% Science Blue and sky accents (actions and highlights). Science Blue is an accent with authority — when everything is blue, nothing is.

Typesetting rules

  • Sentence case for headlines and buttons’ surrounding copy; Title Case only for product names and CTA labels (“Request Demo”).
  • Two weights are enough: Medium for reading, Bold for hierarchy. Don’t introduce Light or Black.
  • Keep headline measure ≤ 14 words; body measure 60–76 characters.
  • The serif never appears in product UI, buttons, or forms — editorial surfaces only.
  • Em dashes without spaces (“transaction—so teams…”), matching site copy.

07Imagery & illustration

Real estate, set in a friendly cosmos

SkySlope’s most distinctive visual asset is its space world: astronauts, UFOs, shooting stars, and moonlit mountain ranges rendered in flat vector with neon glows — always in service of a product story.

The space world (hero & feature illustrations)

  • Setting: deep-space backdrops on Mirage/space gradients with starfields and shooting-star streaks.
  • Cast: friendly astronaut characters, UFOs beaming up documents, crystals/gems, moons and mountains — tech doing the heavy lifting with a wink.
  • Light: neon glows in violet #533DB1, magenta-pink, and cyan/sky #079EFF–#4EAFFF; a single strong light source (beams, halos).
  • Style: flat vector with soft gradient shading — no outlines, no 3-D renders, no gritty texture.
  • Purpose: the illustration always dramatizes a product action (e.g., a UFO beaming signatures onto a contract for DigiSign).

Product UI imagery

  • Real screenshots, framed in white cards with 8–12 px radius and the soft cool shadow; often floating over dark scenes or overlapping section edges.
  • Screenshots are current, legible, and staged with realistic data — never blurred or skewed into deep perspective.

People & photography

  • Editorial/blog surfaces use warm, natural photography of agents, homes, and teams — candid over corporate.
  • Flat-vector people illustrations (blue-dominant wardrobe) appear in feature stories and social cards.
Do
  • Keep space scenes on dark gradients; keep daylight scenes on white.
  • Use one hero illustration per page; let white space carry the rest.
  • Tie every illustration to a concrete product benefit.
  • Keep glows in the violet–cyan family.
Don’t
  • Drop space illustrations onto white backgrounds without their scene.
  • Mix in stock 3-D blobs, isometric office clip-art, or photo-collage.
  • Add red/orange sci-fi menace — this cosmos is optimistic.
  • Let decoration outshine the product story.
08Iconography

Simple, rounded, monochrome

Icons are functional wayfinding, not decoration. The site ships Font Awesome (solid/brands) plus simple custom glyphs.

  • Style: solid or 2 px-stroke line icons with rounded terminals; geometric, no detail noise.
  • Color: single color only — Slope Slate or Science Blue on light; white or Light Sky on dark.
  • Sizing: 16 / 20 / 24 px grid; optical centering inside 40–48 px tap targets.
  • Checkmarks introduce list items in feature copy (observed pattern), set in Science Blue or Success green.
  • Don’t mix icon families in one view, and never use emoji as UI icons.

Confident, plain-spoken, in motion

SkySlope writes like a great transaction coordinator talks: sure of the process, allergic to fluff, and always moving the deal forward.

10Writing style

The mechanics

Naming & capitalization

TermRule
SkySlopeOne word, two capitals. Never “Skyslope,” “SkySlope’s platform” is fine.
DigiSign, SkyTC, SkySight, SkyHighInternal capitals exactly as shown.
Forms, Offers, Books, Breeze, AyceCapitalized as product names; pair with SkySlope on first mention (“SkySlope Forms”).
Smart SuiteTwo words, both capitalized.
real estate, brokerage, transaction managementLowercase generic terms.
agents, brokers, TCs, auditorsLowercase; “TC” = transaction coordinator, spell out on first use in long-form.

Conventions

  • Headlines and UI copy in sentence case; CTA button labels in Title Case (1–3 words).
  • Numerals for stats and counts: “900,000+ professionals,” “24/7 support,” “founded in 2011.”
  • Em dash with no surrounding spaces for emphasis breaks — exactly like this.
  • Second person by default (“your agents,” “your transactions”); first-person plural for the company (“we’re obsessed with our customers”).
  • Avoid: solution(s), synergy, leverage (as a verb), robust, seamless, revolutionary, “simply.”
  • Exclamation points: at most one per page, ideally zero.

Practice

  • Body text ≥ 16 px (the site’s default is 19 px); never grayer than Gray 600 on white.
  • Visible focus on everything interactive: 3 px ring rgba(0,89,218,.35).
  • Never encode meaning in color alone — pair status colors with icons or labels.
  • Alt text tells the product story of an illustration (“Astronaut reviewing a checklist”), or is empty for pure decoration.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: starfields and floating animations must settle.
  • Pill buttons keep ≥ 44 px touch height on mobile.
