The one-paragraph story

How it all connects▶

The June audit proved SkySlope's channels are all ask and no give. The keyword data shows competitors own the research narrative — Paperless Pipeline owns the TC audience, Dotloop owns brand gravity — while SkySlope wins AI overviews on broker terms. The review platforms show a validation gap (3.9★ Capterra, half the review volume of rivals). The answer: publish the industry's only proprietary transaction data (the give nobody else can copy), deliver it through the personas' actual channels, activate 900K users + sales + execs as distribution, and fix the review surface in parallel. Awareness → credibility → referral, each stage reinforcing the next.

Doc 1 · Social Media Strategy

Turning 3 million transactions into the most shareable data in real estate · Builds on the July 9 proposal + the shared "2026: SkySlope Social Media Engagement" plan
Executive Summary▶
  • Core services stand (July 9 proposal): content strategy, creation, publishing, community management across LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram — 20 hrs/month.
  • The audit's bottom line frames everything: engagement is a content problem, not frequency or paid spend. Channels have been a press-release feed — every post an ask, none a give.
  • What's new: SkySlope sits on one of the largest proprietary datasets in residential real estate ops — 900K+ professionals, 3M+ transactions/yr. Zillow/Redfin own the consumer-data narrative; nobody owns the transaction-process narrative.
  • Recommendation: launch The SkySlope Index — a recurring data franchise converting anonymized aggregate data into headline-grabbing claims that earn links, press, and shares — compounding the SEO and AI-citation work already under way.
Where We're Starting: The June AuditBaseline▶
  • Flat baseline — sampled IG posts: 2–9 likes, zero comments; account reads as a press-release channel.
  • Testimonials win — the two customer Spotlight videos (Century 21 Judge Fite, NextHome) beat every static post. Human voice is the one proven lever.
  • All ask, no give — 100% of sampled posts end in a CTA; captions written like landing pages.
  • QA gaps — the "Johnny" caption on the Davi Bryce video; a lightweight review step fixes this cheaply.
  • Hashtags/paid aren't the lever — generic tags don't drive discovery; boosting flat content buys impressions nobody reacts to.

Success = engagement rate (likes + comments + shares / reach) vs this baseline, month over month — not reach or follower count.

The Big Idea: The SkySlope IndexHero franchise▶
  • Monthly Stat Drop — one bold, counterintuitive number as a shareable graphic.
  • Quarterly "State of the Transaction" — 5–8 stats with charts; carousel + blog + email; pitched to Inman, HousingWire, RISMedia.
  • Annual "Your Year in Closings" — Wrapped-style industry recap; phase 2: personalized agent stats in-product.

Example claims (illustrative — every claim validated & approved before publishing):

  • "The average closing involves [X] documents and [Y] signatures."
  • "Deals in Texas close [N] days faster than California — the state leaderboard."
  • "[X]% of transactions hit a compliance flag before closing. Top 3 culprits, ranked."
  • "The most common day to go under contract is Tuesday. Least common? Sunday."

Why it wins: data content compounds the SEO/interlinking work and AI-answer visibility (SkySlope already ranks #1 in some ChatGPT answers); no competitor publishes proprietary industry data; agents share stats that make their world legible.

Data Governance (Read This First)Guardrails▶
  • No PII — aggregate and anonymized only; never addresses, client/agent names, brokerage-identifiable figures, or deal-level detail.
  • Minimum thresholds — no stat from a slice small enough to identify anyone.
  • Legal sign-off — every claim reviewed by SkySlope legal/compliance before first publication; methodology note per recurring stat.
  • Access process — SkySlope's team defines exports; Click Shift works from approved aggregates, never raw records.

This is what makes the numbers press-citable — protection and credibility are the same move.

Platforms & Cadence▶
  • LinkedIn — the insight channel (6–8/mo). Index stats + quarterly reports as document/carousel posts; 2–3 execs posting personally 1×/week with the Page resharing; partnership news lands here first.
  • Instagram — the end-user channel (4–6/mo). Lead each week with a testimonial or relatable pain-point post before any feature content; drop generic hashtags; waitlist/launch content is paid-only.
  • Facebook — the community channel (4–6/mo). Participate in 2–3 brokerage/RE-tech Groups as named humans; longer captions OK; cross-post testimonials.
  • TikTok — phase-2 test. Longer-format tolerance suits office-hours; launch with intentional seeding to the right audience, no friends-and-family padding.

Paid rule: boost only formats with proven organic engagement (testimonials; Index once validated) — never waitlist/launch posts.

Quarter-end months weight toward the State of the Transaction report.

Audience Definition▶
  • Economic buyers — broker-owners, ops leaders, admins at mid-size→enterprise residential brokerages & franchises (US/CA) who own the back-office decision.
  • Users & champions — TCs (in-house and independent) and agents: rarely sign the contract, but make or break adoption, renewal, word-of-mouth — and they're the largest searching audience in the data.
  • Ecosystem — MLSs/associations, franchise leadership, RE-tech reviewers, TC communities, peer brokers: the referral network that validates the shortlist.

Defining trait: a small, dense, hyper-networked industry. Social here isn't broadcast media — it's the industry's group chat.

The Audience Growth & Engagement EngineFixing low follows▶

Content alone won't fix a distribution problem. Follows and engagement are low relative to a 900K-user base — so the engine builds the audience deliberately:

  • 6.1 Convert the base you own — follow prompts with a reason ("follow for the monthly Index stat") in-product, in onboarding emails, support-resolution emails, webinar end-cards, newsletter footers. Feature-to-follow flywheel: Customer Spotlights and "TC of the Month" make being featured the incentive. Annual Wrapped personal stats = the single strongest follow driver available.
  • 6.2 Sales, CS & leadership as the distribution layer — the first 100 engagements are internal: every AE/CSM/exec shares & comments within the first hour (weekly share kit, angles not scripts). Sales uses Index stats as non-pitch touches; engagers become warm signals. 2–3 execs posting weekly; the Page harvests the followers.
  • 6.3 Borrow audiences — MLS/franchise/integration partner co-marketing and tag exchanges; value-first participation in TC groups and Reddit as named humans; TC-educator micro-influencers walking through real workflows.
  • 6.4 Engagement mechanics — every post engineered for a response (polls, "tag a TC who…", first-line hooks); reply to every comment/DM in 24h; product mention in the reply, not the caption; paid only against warm audiences (customer lists, retargeting, lookalikes).
  • 6.5 Targets (2 quarters) — engagement rate above June baseline within 6 weeks; comments >0 on 80% of posts in 90 days; follower growth tracked by % from named customer/prospect accounts; +40 fresh G2/Capterra reviews in 6 months (Capterra first, non-CA voices); 10 employee advocates weekly, 2 exec voices, 3 sustained community groups.

Inspiration

Hook formulas

📊 Data & Index posts

  • "The average closing runs on [number]." — one stat, huge type, nothing else on the graphic.
  • Leaderboards: "Fastest-closing states, ranked." People argue with rankings in comments — that's the point.
  • Counterintuitive beats impressive: "Sunday is the rarest day to go under contract" outperforms "we processed 3M transactions."
  • Always end carousels with the methodology slide — credibility is the moat.

🎬 Testimonial / Spotlight

  • Open on the customer's result, not their name: "She closes 40 files a month. Here's how."
  • Cut a 15-sec vertical teaser from every long interview; full video to YouTube.
  • Caption = one quote from the video, nothing more.

😅 Zero-CTA relatable

  • POV formats: "POV: the lender says 'quick question' at 4:58pm Friday."
  • Tag-a-colleague: "Tag a TC who's survived a 40-document closing."
  • Polls: "Worst part of closing week?" — four options, all painful.
  • RULE: no link, no pitch, no product name in the caption. Product lives in a comment reply if anyone asks.

🎓 Education / product

  • Lead with the pain, not the feature: "Chasing signatures at 9pm?" then the 3-click answer.
  • Screen-record real workflows — agents trust screens, not renders.

Automation Policy

⚠ Read before automating anything

Third-party auto-DM tools and follow/unfollow bots violate the terms of service of Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn. Platforms actively detect this (action blocks, shadow restrictions, suspensions — LinkedIn permanently bans accounts for it). For a brand rebuilding credibility, an action-blocked account or a spam-flagged DM is a far bigger setback than slow organic growth. Recommendation: do not automate DMs or follow/unfollow activity with third-party tools — full stop.
